> THE PROBLEM

Important public reports exist. They are just hard to find, hard to use, and hard to link to.

Governments publish vast amounts of high-quality information on matters of real public importance: official inquiries, investigations, and reports produced with serious resources, expert authorship, and often subpoena powers.

Yet much of this material is effectively hidden. Scattered across obscure websites, locked in PDFs, poorly indexed, and impossible to link to at the level that matters: the specific finding, paragraph, or piece of evidence.

> THE FIX

Reports that Matter makes important public-interest reports usable on the web.

We make reports:

  • DISCOVERABLE
  • READABLE
  • LINKABLE
  • SEARCHABLE

No more dead-end PDFs. No more maze-like government sites. Just content you can navigate, cite, and share.

> EVIDENCE_01: US SENATE FINANCE REPORT

One of the primary sources on the 2008 financial crisis. Accessing it is a case study in failure.

WHAT HAPPENS TODAY:

  1. Locate correct Senate subcommittee site.
  2. Realize there is no search.
  3. Find the list of reports is a PDF.
  4. Discover links in that PDF are broken.
  5. Arrive at multiple 100MB+ PDFs.
  6. Find text versions are unusable.

> EVIDENCE_02: THE SAVILLE INQUIRY (UK)

Examined the killing of civilians on Bloody Sunday. A bespoke website exists, but is functionally broken.

WHAT HAPPENS TODAY:

  1. "Reports" page has no links.
  2. Page numbers are plain text.
  3. Downloads are opaque + fragmented.
  4. Alternative formats: email someone.

The issue is not absence of information, but absence of usable access.

> SYSTEMIC FAILURE

The pattern repeats: publish once, structure poorly, allow decay. These reports are published as if the web did not exist.
