REPORTS THAT MATTER

Important public reports exist. They are just hard to find, use, and link to.

// THE PROBLEM

Governments publish vast amounts of high-quality information on matters of real public importance: official inquiries, investigations, and reports produced with serious resources, expert authorship, and often subpoena powers.


Yet much of this material is effectively hidden. Scattered across obscure websites, locked in PDFs, poorly indexed, and impossible to link to at the level that matters: the specific finding, paragraph, or piece of evidence.

// THE FIX

Reports that Matter makes public-interest reports usable on the web. We make them:

  • DISCOVERABLE
  • READABLE
  • LINKABLE
  • SEARCHABLE

No more dead-end PDFs. No more maze-like government sites. Just content you can navigate, cite, and share.

// EVIDENCE_01: US SENATE FINANCE REPORT

A primary source on the 2008 financial crisis. Accessing it is a case study in structural failure.

FAILURE ANALYSIS:

  1. Locate correct Senate subcommittee site.
  2. Realize there is no search function.
  3. Find the list of reports is a PDF.
  4. Discover links in that PDF are broken.
  5. Arrive at multiple 100MB+ PDFs.
  6. Find text versions are unusable.

// EVIDENCE_02: THE SAVILLE INQUIRY (UK)

Examined the killing of civilians on Bloody Sunday. Its bespoke website is functionally broken.

FAILURE ANALYSIS:

  1. "Reports" page contains no real links.
  2. Page numbers are plain text, not navigable.
  3. Downloads are opaque and fragmented.
  4. Alternative formats require emailing someone.

[FIG.2A] The bespoke, yet unusable, website.

// CONCLUSION

The pattern repeats: publish once, structure poorly, allow decay. These reports are published as if the web did not exist. Making them usable is a small but foundational step toward a better-informed public discourse.
