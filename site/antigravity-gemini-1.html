Public Record Initiative 2026.01

The knowledge is there.
It is just inaccessible.

Governments publish vast amounts of high-quality information: official inquiries, investigations, and reports produced with serious resources, expert authorship, and subpoena powers.

Yet much of this material is effectively hidden in plain sight.

Scattered across broken websites, locked inside gargantuan PDFs, and poorly indexed by search engines, these reports are almost impossible to reference at the level that actually matters: the specific finding, the paragraph, the evidence.

"We treat our most important public records as if the web did not exist."

Reports that Matter makes the public record web-native.

A Demonstration of Failure

Case I: The US Senate Financial Crisis Report

A primary source for modern history, buried under archaic navigation. To find a specific revelation, a researcher must navigate a maze of broken links and massive, fragmented files.

Case II: The Saville Inquiry (UK)

An inquiry of profound political importance. While a bespoke site exists, many page references remain plain text and alternative formats require direct correspondence with Archives.
