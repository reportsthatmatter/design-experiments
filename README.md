## Prompting approach (Codex)

- Asked for quick mockups of a long-scroll landing page using the project brief and draft narrative.
- Requested three options: two specified styles (documentary minimal and civic tech modern) plus a wildcard.
- Emphasized elegance and a serious, editorial tone over marketing or conversion-first patterns.
- Optional use of existing screenshots in `site/assets/images/`.

# Appendix: Design Process Log

This document chronicles the brainstorming and refinement process for the initial landing page mockups.

## 1. Initial Brainstorming Request

The process began with a request to quickly mock up two designs, but to first brainstorm several stylistic directions.

> **User Prompt:**
> what i want you to do now is to quickly mock up in `site` subfolder as gemini-1.html and gemini-2.html two designs for the front page. move quickly for now - you can use images in @site/assets/** e.g. [senate-screenshot-1.png 1500x1125] but this optional. I would recommend start by brainstorming a few options for the stylistic direction we could go e.g. newspaper or civictech etc and giving me 3-5 options to choose from ... you can also include a wildcard.

## 2. Proposed Stylistic Directions

The first response was a list of abstract concepts and their characteristics.

> **Agent Response:**
> 1.  **The "Digital Archive" Look:**
>     *   **Concept:** Clean, academic, and authoritative. Think of a modern university library or a service like JSTOR.
>
> 2.  **The "Modern Civic Tech" Look:**
>     *   **Concept:** Accessible, clear, and user-focused, in the style of ProPublica or the GOV.UK Design System.
>
> 3.  **The "Long-form Journalism" Look:**
>     *   **Concept:** A high-impact, narrative-driven page, like an interactive feature from the New York Times or The Guardian.
>
> 4.  **The "Declassified Document" Look (Wildcard):**
>     *   **Concept:** A stylized, thematic approach that presents the content as if it were part of a newly unearthed collection of official files.

## 3. Request for Concrete Inspiration

The initial abstract directions were not distinctive enough, leading to a request for real-world examples.

> **User Prompt:**
> these don't seem that amazing to me or distinctive. any sites out there you could recommend that would be a good good and could serve as inspiration ...

## 4. Inspirational Website Analysis

In response, a list of inspirational websites was provided, linking their design language to the project's goals.

> **Agent Response:**
> 1.  **The Intercept ([https://theintercept.com/](https://theintercept.com/))**
>     *   **Why it's inspiring:** It has a bold, newspaper-like quality but feels modern and digital... a "Modern Newsroom" aesthetic.
>
> 2.  **The Pudding ([https://pudding.cool/](https://pudding.cool/))**
>     *   **Why it's inspiring:** The gold standard for visual, data-driven storytelling... their approach to weaving narrative, images, and data is best-in-class.
>
> 3.  **Macaulay Library ([https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/](https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/))**
>     *   **Why it's inspiring:** A pure "Digital Archive" but executed beautifully... creates a sense of scholarly authority and exploration.
>
> 4.  **The Markup ([https://themarkup.org/](https://themarkup.org/))**
>     *   **Why it's inspiring:** They have a unique "Show Your Work" methodology... creates a powerful sense of transparency and evidence.
>
> 5.  **Readymag "Brutalism" ([https://readymag.com/examples/brutalism/](https://readymag.com/examples/brutalism/))**
>     *   **Why it's inspiring (Wildcard):** A raw, challenging style that exposes the underlying structure... can be very effective for projects that want to feel un-designed, honest, and direct.

## 5. Final Direction Selection

The user then selected two of the concrete examples to use as foundational approaches.

> **User Prompt:**
> create the two options using 4 and 5 approaches.

## 6. Final Refinement: Emphasis on Elegance

After the initial mockups were created, a final, crucial piece of feedback was given to guide the visual polish.

> **User Prompt:**
> I want it to look beautiful and stunning. I think sites that are really elegant and beautiful are really impactful, so that's important to me. Even if we go minimalist, can you really make these elegant and beautiful.

This directive led to the final refined versions of `gemini-1.html` ("Modern Editorial") and `gemini-2.html` ("Refined Minimalism").
